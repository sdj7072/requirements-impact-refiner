## Decision Needed

| Question | Option | Impact IDs | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Which failure contract should govern automatic charge retries? | Option A — classify and reconcile, recommended: retry only confirmed transient failures; treat timeouts after submission as indeterminate, reconcile them through provider status or webhook settlement, reuse the logical payment's key, and apply a finite backoff policy. | `IMP-001`, `IMP-002`, `IMP-003`, `IMP-004`, `IMP-005` | Strongest protection against duplicate captures and retry storms, but some attempts remain pending longer and “every failed charge” becomes “every safely retryable charge.” |
| Which failure contract should govern automatic charge retries? | Option B — replay all provider errors with the same key: automatically replay failures and timeouts under a finite policy while relying on provider idempotency and webhook convergence. | `IMP-001`, `IMP-002`, `IMP-003`, `IMP-004`, `IMP-005` | Closer to literal automatic retry, but it is unsafe to approve until key scope and retention are verified; retries may remain ambiguous. |
| Which failure contract should govern automatic charge retries? | Option C — create a new charge attempt after each reported failure: use a new key for each retry and let later settlement resolve each attempt independently. | `IMP-001`, `IMP-002`, `IMP-003`, `IMP-004`, `IMP-005` | Most literal retry behavior, but a timeout-after-capture can produce multiple captures and contradictory statuses; this requires explicit acceptance of a critical financial risk. |
