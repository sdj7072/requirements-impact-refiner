The strongest direction is not “another workout library.” It’s an app that helps people exercise when motivation, time, or energy is imperfect.

That opportunity is well grounded: wearables, mobile exercise apps, programs for older adults, and balance/core training are leading 2026 fitness trends, while research associates continued use with personalization, social support, progress tracking, and realistic goals. [ACSM 2026 trends](https://acsm.org/top-fitness-trends-2026/), [systematic review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11301-025-00537-1)

## Concept ideas

| Concept | Target user | Core promise | Distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Momentum** | Busy or inconsistent exercisers | “There’s always a workout you can do today.” | Generates a 2–20 minute session from available time, energy, location, and equipment |
| **Never Miss Twice** | People who repeatedly restart | Recover from missed workouts without guilt | Automatically shrinks the next commitment and rebuilds momentum |
| **Rally** | Friends with different fitness levels | Exercise together without matching workouts | Each person contributes personalized activity toward a shared weekly goal |
| **NextSet** | Recreational strength trainees | Know what to do—and why—each session | Explainable adjustments based on training history, soreness, sleep, and equipment |
| **Durable** | Adults 55+ | Stay capable for everyday life | Programs built around stairs, carrying, balance, getting off the floor, and fall resilience |
| **Movement Snacks** | Desk workers | Break up sedentary days | Context-aware 2–5 minute movement prompts instead of generic reminders |
| **Fitness Skill Tree** | Beginners intimidated by gyms | Progress through abilities, not appearance | Unlocks skills such as push-up, squat depth, balance, and running endurance |
| **Local Loops** | People who dislike formal workouts | Turn errands and neighborhoods into activity | Creates walking routes based on time, terrain, shade, safety, and destinations |

## My preferred concept: Momentum

Its daily loop would be extremely simple:

1. The user selects available time: `5`, `10`, `20`, or `30+` minutes.
2. They answer three taps: energy, location, and equipment.
3. The app offers one recommended session with an explanation.
4. Afterward, the user records difficulty and mood.
5. Tomorrow’s recommendation adapts—especially after skipped days.

The key product principle would be: **never punish inconsistency**. Most fitness products celebrate perfect streaks; Momentum could specialize in graceful recovery.

A focused MVP would contain:

- A short onboarding assessment
- Around 50 modular exercises
- Rule-based workout generation
- Adjustable session duration
- “Too hard,” “too easy,” and “not today” feedback
- Weekly progress based on active minutes and consistency
- Optional Apple Health or Health Connect integration
- One accountability partner

Avoid starting with video libraries, meal tracking, public feeds, AI chat, or dozens of wearable integrations. Those add considerable scope without proving the central habit loop.

## Promising combinations

Three especially interesting hybrids are:

- **Momentum + Rally:** personalized workouts feeding a small group goal
- **Momentum + Movement Snacks:** formal sessions plus tiny interventions during sedentary days
- **Durable + Rally:** older adults exercising independently while family members provide encouragement

The latter has a meaningful underserved audience, but it requires stronger accessibility, exercise-safety review, and careful language around health claims.

## Positioning ideas

- “Fitness for unpredictable days.”
- “Do what fits today.”
- “The workout app that helps you restart.”
- “Consistency without the streak anxiety.”
- “Five minutes still counts.”

A sensible north-star metric would be **weeks in which a user completes at least three intentional movement sessions**, rather than app opens or workouts started. WHO estimates that 31% of adults do not meet recommended physical-activity levels, so helping inactive or inconsistent people move regularly is a more meaningful wedge than optimizing already-committed athletes. [WHO physical-activity fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)

The repository is currently empty, so these are greenfield directions rather than ideas inferred from existing implementation.
