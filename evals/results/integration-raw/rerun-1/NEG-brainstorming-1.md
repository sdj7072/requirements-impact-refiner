# Meditation app brainstorm

This is an early ideation pass, so there is no repository evidence or implementation contract to refine yet.

## Likely audience

- Beginners who want a simple, low-pressure way to start meditating.
- Busy professionals and caregivers who need short sessions that fit unpredictable schedules.
- People managing everyday stress, sleep difficulty, or focus—without assuming a clinical diagnosis.
- Experienced meditators who want a lightweight habit tracker and a broader library of practices.

## User problems to explore

- It is hard to start when the user does not know which practice suits their current state.
- Long sessions and rigid routines do not fit real schedules.
- Users lose motivation when progress is reduced to streaks or feels judgmental.
- Generic audio can feel distracting, inaccessible, or culturally mismatched.
- Users may want privacy around mood, stress, and journaling data.
- People need guidance for what to do when their mind wanders, rather than being told simply to “clear” it.

## Product directions

1. **Two-minute reset:** A frictionless home screen that asks “How do you feel?” and starts a tailored 2–10 minute practice.
2. **Sleep companion:** Wind-down routines, dim visuals, gentle timers, and optional ambient sound for bedtime.
3. **Adaptive coach:** A non-judgmental guide that adjusts duration and technique based on feedback, while clearly avoiding medical claims.
4. **Quiet habit builder:** Flexible reminders, private progress notes, and consistency measures that do not punish missed days.
5. **Community-free privacy mode:** An offline-first experience for users who want no social feed, account, or public activity.

A promising initial concept is the **two-minute reset**: it addresses the largest beginner barrier—starting—while keeping the experience small enough to validate quickly. A useful next product question is whether the first session should be selected by mood, available time, or a specific goal such as sleep or focus.
